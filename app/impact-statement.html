What is an impact statement?

It is your opportunity to describe how the betrayal has affected your inner world and your life. It is not about proving every fact, punishing your partner or deciding today whether the relationship will continue. Its purpose is to help your partner understand your lived experience and create space for empathy and accountability.

Before you begin

A helpful boundary

Write about the impact on you. Questions that still need factual answers can be kept on a separate page for a planned disclosure process. You do not have to solve unanswered questions inside this statement.

From Before to After

These prompts can help you describe the change caused by the betrayal. Use only those that feel relevant.

What I Need You to Understand

Keep the focus on your experience

Try to use “I” language: “I felt…”, “I began to believe…”, “My body responded…”. Clear, direct language is powerful. You do not need to minimise the impact, and insults are unlikely to help your experience be heard.

Building My Statement

Use this as a suggested flow. Your statement can sound like you and does not have to follow every section.

  1. BeforeWhat I believed about us, you, myself and our future before discovery.
  2. DiscoveryWhat discovering the betrayal was like emotionally, physically and psychologically.
  3. The ongoing impactHow it has affected trust, identity, daily life, family, work, intimacy and relationships.
  4. The lossesWhat I am grieving, including things that cannot simply be put back as they were.
  5. What I need you to understandThe meaning and depth of the injury from my point of view.
  6. What I need going forwardWhat consistent actions may begin to rebuild safety and trust. These are needs and boundaries, not a guarantee of reconciliation.

My statement

Use as many writing areas as you need. Each expands as you type.

Before Sharing

An impact statement is usually best shared in a planned therapeutic setting, once both people have enough support to remain present.

Remember

This statement does not have to decide the future of the relationship. It cannot rebuild trust by itself. Its role is to give your experience a voice. Safety and trust are rebuilt through honest, consistent actions over time.
